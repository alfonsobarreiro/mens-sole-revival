## What morning heel pain actually is

{/* Section 1 (~250 words).
    Prompt: describe plantar fasciitis in mechanical terms. The plantar
    fascia is a thick band running from the heel to the base of the
    toes; it acts like a bowstring holding up the arch. When it's
    inflamed at the heel attachment, the first tension load of the day
    (weight on the arch) hurts sharply.

    Cite: NIH StatPearls plantar fasciitis (already in the pillar's
    sources list) https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK431073/ */}

## Why it's worst in the first steps

{/* Section 2 (~250 words).
    Prompt: explain the "windlass mechanism" at rest. Overnight the

    first-steps pain that eases) is nearly pathognomonic for plantar

## Is it plantar fasciitis, or something else?

{/* Section 3 (~350 words) — this is a diagnostic self-check.
    Prompt: differential per condition:

## What works, ranked by evidence

{/* Section 4 (~400 words).
    Prompt: rank interventions by strength of evidence.

    1. Calf + plantar fascia stretch protocol (strongest evidence for
       first-line treatment; ~80% of cases resolve in 6–12 weeks with
       consistent stretching).
    2. Night splint (evidence is mixed but positive; keeps fascia at
       length overnight so morning stretch isn't cold).
    3. Orthotic support (OTC or custom; evidence stronger for men on
       feet all day).
    4. Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (for chronic PF that doesn't
       respond to conservative care; podiatrist-administered).
    5. Corticosteroid injection (short-term relief only; increases
       fascia rupture risk if repeated).

    Cite: NIH StatPearls for the evidence hierarchy. Note that most
    men over 40 will get 80% of the benefit from #1 + #2 + shoe fix.

    Link to: [[what-your-dress-shoes-are-doing-to-your-feet]] on the
    footwear point, and to /reviews/superfeet-blue-insoles for the
    orthotic option. */}

## The 4-week morning protocol

{/* Section 5 (~300 words) — actionable.
    Prompt: give a specific 4-week protocol the reader can start today.

    Week 1: Calf stretch (both legs) + plantar fascia stretch (rolled
    towel under toes) BEFORE getting out of bed. 30 seconds each, 3
    rounds. Then the same sequence after any period of sitting > 20
    minutes.

    Week 2: Add wall calf stretch (bent knee variation) 3x per day.
    Same in-bed protocol.

    Week 3: Layer in single-leg calf raises (10 reps per side, 2
    rounds) once daily. Continue morning protocol.

    Week 4: If pain is >50% improved, add barefoot toe curls (grip a
    towel with toes, 10 reps, 2 rounds). If pain hasn't improved by
    week 4, escalate: consider a night splint and/or podiatrist visit.

    Frame: this is minimum-effective-dose. Add more only after the
    baseline habit sticks.

    Callout box: Cross-link to [[5-minute-routine]] for the daily
    habit anchor. */}

## What doesn't work (and where the money goes wrong)

{/* Section 6 (~200 words).
    Prompt:
      · Expensive shoes without stretching (footwear alone won't fix a
        shortened calf + fascia).
      · Ice alone (small analgesic, no tissue-load benefit).
      · Repeated cortisone injections (masks pain, weakens fascia).
      · Deep massage of the plantar surface during acute inflammation
        (can worsen).
      · Waiting for it to resolve on its own (subacute PF can persist
        > 12 months untreated).

    Voice note: no aphorisms; state the evidence plainly. */}

## When to see a podiatrist

{/* Section 7 (~150 words) — the escalation criteria.
    Prompt:
      · Pain doesn't improve after 6 weeks of consistent stretching.
      · Pain becomes constant (not just first-steps).
      · Numbness, tingling, or burning enters the picture.
      · Heel pain with visible swelling or warmth.
      · Diabetes + any change in foot pain (always urgent).
      · Pain follows a fall or awkward step (rule out stress fracture).

    Link to /doctor-prep at the end for the checklist to bring. */}
