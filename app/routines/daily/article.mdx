# The Nightly 5-Minute Foot-Care Checklist for Men Over 40

Wash. Dry between toes. Inspect. Moisturize. Done before your phone hits the charger.

That's the whole routine. This page is the checklist version, meant to be printed and taped above the bathroom sink. For the essay version with the mechanism behind each step, see [the 5-minute daily routine guide](/guides/5-minute-routine). If you already know why and just want the sheet, you're in the right place.

---

## Who this routine is for

- Every man over 40 who wants a boring maintenance layer that prevents most foot problems from starting.
- Men who have never had a formal foot-care routine and want the minimum-effective-dose habit.
- Men recovering from a plantar fasciitis, athlete's foot, or cracked-heel flare who want to prevent recurrence.

## Who this is NOT for

- Diabetic feet with reduced sensation: your daily inspection is longer and more careful. See a podiatrist for a personalized routine.
- Anyone with an active wound, blister, or fresh cut: address the wound first, resume routine after healing.

---

## Why 5 minutes is enough

The foot-care research is unusually clear that consistency beats intensity for this specific task. A 5-minute nightly routine prevents 80 to 90 percent of common foot problems (athlete's foot, cracked heels, ingrown nail edges, small callus buildup, missed early-warning changes) that men over 40 develop. A 30-minute weekly session does not; the moisture-loss and fungal-growth mechanisms happen daily and need daily contact.

Anchor it to something you already do every night (brush teeth, phone-to-charger, shower-off) and the habit locks in within 3 weeks for most men.

---

## The Protocol

Five steps. Do them in order. About 5 minutes total.

### 1. Wash (60 seconds)

Warm water and mild soap. Rub between the toes with your fingers, not just water. Rinse. If you shower daily, your feet already got the wash step; skip to step 2. If not, do a bathroom-sink wash before the rest.

### 2. Dry between the toes (30 seconds)

Toe-by-toe. Not a general dab. The space between the 4th and 5th toe is the darkest, dampest microclimate on your body; it's where 80 percent of athlete's foot infections start. Fungi need moisture and warmth to grow; dry toe webs starve them.

**Common mistake:** drying only the top of the foot. The problem is between the toes.

### 3. Inspect (15 seconds)

A quick visual scan of both feet. What you're looking for:

- Color change (redness, purple, black spots)
- New callus buildup
- Small cuts, blisters, or peeling skin
- Nail edges lifting or discoloring
- Any spot that hurts to press

If you catch things at week 1, they're 10x easier to fix than at week 4. This is the whole point of the daily inspection.

**Diabetic modification:** longer inspection with a mirror for the sole; check the space between every toe. Same 15 seconds is not enough with reduced sensation.

### 4. Moisturize (2 minutes)

Urea-based cream, 10 to 25 percent. Apply to damp (not wet) feet. Focus on heels, sides of the foot, and any dry patches. Skip between the toes; that's where you want dryness, not more moisture.

Why urea: it both moisturizes AND softens callus. Two jobs in one product. Cheaper alternatives (petroleum jelly, plain lotion) moisturize but don't touch callus. If you have cracked heels, step up to 40 percent urea for a 2-week repair phase, then back to 10 to 25 for maintenance.

**Sock trick:** if you have cracked heels or very dry skin, pull cotton socks on right after the cream and sleep in them. The occlusion multiplies the cream's effect 3 to 5x.

### 5. Nail check (30 seconds, weekly-ish)

Not a nightly trim; a nightly glance. Most men over 40 need a trim every 2 to 3 weeks. What the daily glance catches: a nail that grew unevenly, a rough edge that will snag on a sock, a color change that suggests fungus starting.

If a trim is needed, do it before bed (never in a hurry), straight across (never rounded), and file the corners smooth.

## Weekly and monthly additions

The 5 minutes daily is the foundation. On top of it:

- **Weekly ([the Sunday reset](/routines/weekly)):** 20 minutes, foot soak + callus knock-down + heel cream with socks overnight.
- **Monthly:** honest look at your shoe rotation. Any shoe over 400 miles for runners, over 12 months for daily wear, gets retired. Old shoes lose the structural support that prevents most of the compensations that cause pain.
- **Quarterly:** re-measure your foot at end of day. Feet spread and lengthen with age; a shoe that fit at 40 may not at 45.

---

## What to skip (and common mistakes)

**Nightly nail trims.** Trimming too often causes ingrown risk and nail-bed sensitivity. Glance nightly, trim when needed.

**Aggressive callus removal every night.** Nightly pumice or grater wears skin thinner than it needs to be. Callus is the body protecting itself from repeated pressure; nightly aggressive removal signals the body to build it back thicker. Once a week is the ceiling.

**Skipping the between-toes dry because it feels weird.** This is the single highest-leverage 30 seconds in the entire routine. Do it.

**Applying cream to soaking-wet feet.** Cream sits on the water film instead of penetrating skin. Damp, not wet.

**Doing the routine "when you remember."** The habit science is unambiguous: anchor to an existing habit (brush teeth, phone-to-charger) or the routine erodes within 2 weeks. Anchoring is the whole retention mechanism.

## When to escalate

Most of what the daily routine catches is self-manageable. Some findings need a podiatrist:

- Nail with lifting, color change (yellow/green/brown streaks), or crumbly texture: possible fungus.
- Any dark spot under a nail (rule out melanoma): urgent.
- Cracks that bleed or don't close within 2 weeks of consistent moisturizing.
- Numbness or reduced sensation on any part of the foot.
- Diabetes plus any new finding.

For any of the above, bring the [doctor-prep checklist](/doctor-prep) so the visit stays focused.

---

## Bottom line

Five minutes, every night, anchored to an existing habit. Wash, dry between toes, inspect, moisturize. Add a nail trim when the glance shows it's needed.

If you do this from age 40 through 60, you avoid 80 percent of the foot problems your peers develop. Not from magic; from catching the small stuff at week 1 instead of week 4.
